# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

STBcheck is a tool for **lawful IPTV management and diagnostics**: checking portals you are authorized to use, discovering channels on your own subscriptions, and playing streams you have legitimate access to. This project exists only insofar as it serves that purpose. Participation in this community means agreeing to keep it that way.

## Scope

Applies to all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, documentation, and any representation of the project elsewhere.

## Rules

### 1. Legal use only

- Use STBcheck exclusively with portals and services you have **authorization** to access — your own subscriptions or explicit permission from the operator.
- Do not use, promote, or facilitate access to pirated streams, cracked credentials, MAC spoofing against providers you have no agreement with, or stolen account data.
- Do not share portal URLs, MAC addresses, serials, or tokens tied to real servers in issues, PRs, screenshots, logs, or examples. Use `http://example.invalid/c/` and `00:1A:79:xx:xx:xx` style placeholders.
- You are responsible for compliance with the laws of your jurisdiction. Streaming copyrighted content without a license may be a criminal offense in many countries.

### 2. No abuse tooling

Contributions whose primary purpose is evading provider protections — WAF bypass beyond standard client compatibility, credential brute-forcing, bulk scanning of third-party portals — will be rejected regardless of code quality.

### 3. Be decent

Harassment, hate speech, trolling, personal attacks, and publishing others' private information are grounds for removal. Disagree about code, not about people.

### 4. Security disclosure

Found a vulnerability? Do **not** open a public issue. Follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

The maintainers may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that violate this document, and may temporarily or permanently ban contributors for serious or repeated violations.

Report violations privately via GitHub's report feature on the relevant comment/contribution, or by opening a restricted security advisory. Reports are handled confidentially; repeat-offender decisions are final.
